# Head of Security

> As the Head of Security, you are responsible for the safety of the station, the enforcement of Space Law, and the overall effectiveness of the Security department. Your role can be stressful, demanding, and often thankless, but it is your responsibility to ensure your officers are operating professionally, lawfully, and efficiently. Ensure you understand [Security Overview](../space-law/security-overview.md) and [Space Law](../space-law/crimes-and-punishments.md).

## Quick Reference

| A Good Head of Security...          | A Poor Head of Security...                |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Leads and coordinates officers      | Tries to handle every incident personally |
| Enforces Space Law fairly           | Uses Security for personal agendas        |
| Prioritizes communication           | Operates without informing their team     |
| De-escalates when possible          | Escalates situations unnecessarily        |
| Holds officers accountable          | Ignores misconduct                        |
| Protects lives and station security | Values arrests over crew safety           |
| Remains calm under pressure         | Acts recklessly or emotionally            |

```admonish tip id="k8v2rm"
Your job is not to be the station's strongest officer. Your job is to ensure the Security Department operates effectively, professionally, and within the bounds of Space Law.
```

## Core Responsibilities

* You should maintain regular communication with your officers and delegate responsibilities whenever possible.

* A Security department that communicates effectively will always outperform one that does not.

* Keep yourself informed of ongoing incidents, investigations, and developing threats, as even a missed radio transmission can have serious consequences.

* Your primary role is leadership, coordination, and oversight of the department rather than personally responding to every incident.

## Department Leadership

* You are responsible for ensuring your officers follow Space Law, departmental procedures, and acceptable standards of conduct.

* Officers are expected to remain professional, exercise restraint, and avoid escalating situations unnecessarily.

* If an officer is abusing their authority, acting recklessly, or allowing personal feelings to influence their decisions, it is your responsibility to correct that behavior.

* Set the standard for professionalism, competence, and conduct within the Security Department.

* If members of your department are inexperienced, make reasonable efforts to teach them or assign someone capable of doing so.

## Enforcement and Investigations

* Security exists to enforce Space Law and protect the station, not to dominate it.

* Ensure investigations are conducted fairly, evidence is properly considered, and due process is respected whenever practical.

* Officers should be encouraged to gather information, verify facts, and avoid making assumptions before taking enforcement action.

* Major investigations, sensitive cases, and incidents involving Command personnel should generally receive your oversight.

## De-escalation and Diplomacy

* While Security exists to enforce the law, not every situation needs to end in violence.

* Whenever practical, attempt diplomacy, negotiation, and de-escalation before resorting to force.

* This is especially important during hostage situations, barricades, bomb threats, or other high-risk incidents.

* The preservation of life should remain a priority whenever reasonably possible.

* A successful resolution is not measured by arrests alone. Sometimes the best outcome is the one where nobody gets hurt.

## Emergency Response

* During emergencies, you should coordinate Security's response with Command and ensure your officers are deployed where they are most effective.

* Large-scale threats, hostile boardings, terrorist incidents, riots, and station-wide emergencies often require Security to operate as a coordinated team rather than as individual officers.

* A well-organized Security team will often accomplish more than a Head of Security attempting to solve every problem personally.

* During major emergencies, ensure Command remains informed of Security's assessment of the situation and any resources or assistance that may be required.

## Command Responsibilities

* As a member of Command, you should participate in command discussions and provide Security's perspective on station matters.

* Security decisions often affect every department on the station. Coordinate with Command whenever major incidents, emergencies, or high-profile investigations occur.

* Remember that Security serves the station and its crew. The purpose of law enforcement is to maintain order and safety, not to control the station.

## Professional Standards

* You are expected to set the example for the rest of your department.

* Remain professional, remain calm under pressure, and treat both your officers and the crew with respect.

* Security personnel will often follow the standard you establish.

* Do not use your authority to bypass Space Law, ignore due process, settle personal grievances, or shield officers from accountability.

* The Head of Security should be the department's highest standard of professionalism, not an exception to the rules.

```admonish warning id="m5q8xh"
The Head of Security is entrusted with significant authority. Abuse of that authority can be just as harmful to the station as the criminals Security is meant to stop.
```
